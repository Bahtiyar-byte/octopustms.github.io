# LLMP: Load Lifecycle Management and Payments

LLMP is the idea Octopus is built on. A load is not a row in a spreadsheet and not a folder of PDFs. It is one record that lives from the moment you find it to the moment the money lands, and every person who touches it sees the same thing.

## The eight stops

Every load in Octopus passes the same eight stops, in the same order.

1. **Found.** You search 123Loadboard and DAT from inside Octopus, ranked the way a dispatcher thinks: deadhead first, then rate, broker rating and payment terms.
2. **Booked.** One tap to call or message the broker. Book it and the load is created with the broker, rate and stops already filled in. No retyping.
3. **Dispatched.** You assign a driver and a truck. The load lands in the driver's app with the broker's real rate confirmation.
4. **Moving.** The driver's phone is the tracker. You see every truck on one map, and the broker gets a tracking link instead of a check call.
5. **Delivered.** The rate con, the BOL and the signed POD arrive by email on the load's own address, attach themselves, and are read by AI.
6. **Proven.** Before money moves, Octopus checks the delivery: the right document, signed, matching the load, uploaded by the assigned driver.
7. **Paid.** Settlements for every driver pay type, the factoring package, the invoice synced to QuickBooks. When the money lands, one sound.
8. **Archived.** One complete record, kept forever, exportable any time.

## One record per load

Most carriers run a load across five tools: a load board, a phone, a group chat, an email inbox and an accounting program. The load exists in all of them and completely in none of them. When a broker short-pays, when a driver disputes his settlement, when an auditor asks for the POD from March, you go digging.

In Octopus the load is one record. The rate con that was booked is the rate con the driver saw, is the rate con the invoice was built from, is the rate con in the archive. Nothing is retyped, so nothing drifts.

## Why it's a standard, not a feature

A feature is something one app does. A standard is something everyone can check.

Look at the plate on a truck. One number, and an inspector can see the truck, the owner, the registration and the insurance behind it. The plate doesn't carry the information. It connects it.

LLMP treats the load the same way: one unbreakable record of load, truck, verified driver and documents. Today that record is for carriers. It is how you get paid without arguments. Tomorrow an inspector types one plate number and sees everything connected to it: the load, the truck, who was behind the wheel and what paper they carried.

That is the standard. Octopus is where it lives today, but the point is bigger than one app. When every load has one record that nobody can quietly change, the industry stops arguing about what happened and gets back to hauling.
