# One load, two screens

Ask a driver who gets paid by percentage what his last load paid. Then ask the owner. If the two numbers don't match, you've found the oldest argument in trucking.

## The argument

A driver on 30 percent is promised 30 percent of the load. But 30 percent of what? He gets a text from dispatch: "Dallas to Memphis, $2,400." The broker's rate confirmation said $3,000. Nobody showed it to him. His settlement is correct to the penny, and he is short $180.

He can't prove it. The owner can't disprove it. Trust goes first, then the driver goes, and the owner is back on the phone looking for a new one.

## What Octopus shows instead

In Octopus a load has one number, and both screens show it.

The owner's screen shows load #4821, Dallas → Memphis: broker rate con $3,000, driver pay (30%) $900, Octopus $20.

The driver's screen shows the same load #4821, Dallas → Memphis: broker rate con $3,000, your pay (30%) $900, and the broker's PDF, untouched.

Not a retyped number. The broker's own rate confirmation, the document that was booked, as it arrived. The driver opens it on his own phone. The owner sees the same file.

## Why that ends the argument

Settlements add up because nothing is hidden. The driver sees what the load paid and what his share is, and he can check the math on a napkin. The owner never has to defend a number, because the number came from the broker, not from the owner.

That is the whole feature. It is not complicated. It just hasn't been the default, because trucking software was built for the office, and the cab was never given a screen.

## Honest carriers keep their drivers

A driver who can see the whole load doesn't wonder. He doesn't call his buddy at another company to compare rates. He doesn't leave over a number he was never shown. Good drivers are the hardest thing in this business to find, and showing them the real number is the cheapest way to keep them.
