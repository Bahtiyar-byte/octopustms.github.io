# SASR, explained like your bank

Octopus has one security rule with three parts. We call it SASR, the Strict Access Security Rule, and it rolls out at launch. Here is what it means, in words your bank already taught you.

## Your bank runs on your face

Think about how you move money today. You open the banking app, it looks at your face, and the transfer goes through. No password, no secret question. The bank trusts your face more than anything you could type, because a face can't be guessed, texted to a stranger, or written on a sticky note in the cab.

Facial biometrics is that strong: strong enough to move your money. We built Octopus on the same idea, because a load is money too. A rate con is a promise of three thousand dollars, and the signed POD is the paper that turns the promise into cash.

## Rule one: only approved phones get in

Your bank doesn't let a stranger's phone into your account, even with the right password. Neither do we. The owner approves each phone once. From then on, that phone is a known device. Every other phone is a locked door: it can't see your loads, your rate cons, or your drivers.

## Rule two: the assigned driver's face accepts the load

When you dispatch a load, it goes to one driver, on one approved phone. To accept it, that driver unlocks with his face, the same way he unlocks his bank. Not a password a dispatcher could pass around. Not a PIN written on a fuel receipt. His face, on his phone. No password can stand in for it.

## Rule three: a new face destroys the key

Every approved phone carries its own key, the way a passkey does at Apple, Google and Microsoft. If someone adds a new face to that phone, the key destroys itself. Nothing on that phone works until the owner approves it again. The thief holds a brick.

That is the newest standard the biggest companies in the world use to keep accounts safe, and it is how we swap the key: the same concept, one level up.

## Why security and getting paid fast are the same problem

Here is what most software misses. A stolen load, a fake POD, a double-brokered shipment and a driver who "didn't know the rate" are all the same hole: nobody could prove who was really holding the load. Uncertainty is why money moves slowly in this industry. Everyone checks everything twice, and you wait.

SASR closes the hole. When the POD comes from the assigned driver's approved phone, unlocked by his face, the proof of delivery is also proof of who delivered it. One clean record: load, truck, driver, documents. Whoever sees the whole load gets paid fast, and nobody can steal it.

## What this means on launch day

SASR is rolling out at launch. The owner approves phones, drivers unlock with their face, and the key does its own housekeeping. There is no hardware to buy: the phone your driver already carries is the key. Your bank already taught him how to use it.
